## Wording concerns (v2), per figure, with suggested rewrites

1. **d2** — "a highly capable AI" presupposes the capability that Bender (and partly Marcus) deny; they agree that current systems are unpredictable but would balk at the adjective. Suggested: *"There is no dependable test today that tells you how an AI system will behave in situations its makers never tried."*
2. **d9** — "The ways we make today's AI behave will keep working" assumes they work now. Bender's answer is "they don't work now", which the slider cannot express. Suggested: *"The training methods used to steer today's AI (mostly people rating its answers) will still work once AI is far smarter than the people training it."*
3. **d3** — "will probably jump suddenly": Amodei and Hassabis expect very fast but *continuous* exponential progress and would resist "suddenly" while agreeing with "faster than they can respond". Suggested: *"AI ability will probably advance faster than its makers expect and faster than they can respond."*
4. **d7** — "If advanced AI turns out to be dangerous, our existing institutions... will be enough": Tegmark and Russell believe the *same kind* of institutions (FDA-style, nuclear-style) would suffice if created, but say they do not exist yet, so they answer 5 while agreeing with the optimists' method. Consider adding to plain: *"'Existing' means the rules and bodies we have today, not ones we could create."*
5. **s2** — "build it faster, so the most careful developers get there first": Amodei's actual position is "the careful should be at the frontier", which does not require speed. Suggested: *"If powerful AI is coming anyway, the developers who take safety most seriously should make sure they get there first, even if that means building faster."*
6. **s5** — Amodei and Kokotajlo distinguish "don't fall behind rivals" (yes) from "keep pushing unilaterally" (no); both favour pacing *with* allies while keeping a lead over China. Suggested plain-text addition: *"Assume there is no agreement binding everyone."*
7. **s9** — "whatever the expected benefits" makes Amodei/Hassabis/Aschenbrenner answers hard to place: they accept risk for benefit in general but have never said 1% is fine. Fine as written; flag that lab leaders will cluster near 50 with low confidence.
8. **s11** — Hassabis and Amodei both say "open weights, yes, but not the frontier"; the item's "most powerful" already captures this, but the plain text could say *"the frontier models, not last year's"* so the answer is not read as anti-open-source.
9. **p4** — For Bender and Marcus, "AI acting on its own" is not a real category, so their strong agreement measures rejection of the second clause rather than a ranking of two fears. Suggested plain addition: *"If you think one of these two isn't a real danger at all, answer for the other one."*
10. **p5** — Buterin designed a hardware mechanism precisely to make a pause hold, while insisting it must cover militaries; the item's "major powers agreed" leaves that ambiguous. Suggested plain addition: *"'Agreed' includes their militaries."*
