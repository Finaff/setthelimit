# figures-e: David Sacks — quotes relied on

Companion to `figures-e.json` (generated 2026-09-20, content v4). One section per source, in the order of the JSON `sources` array. Pages were fetched on 20 Sept 2026. X blocks ordinary fetchers, so the three long X posts (sources 0, 6, 8) were read in full through a public mirror API (`api.fxtwitter.com/DavidSacks/status/<id>`), and their opening lines and dates were cross-checked against X's public embed endpoint (`cdn.syndication.twimg.com/tweet-result?id=…`), which returns only the first ~280 characters. Web pages were read through a summarising fetcher that was asked for verbatim quotes; where a quote could only be obtained second-hand, the section says so.

**Status.** Sacks was the White House Special Advisor for AI and Crypto ("AI & Crypto Czar") from January 2025. He no longer holds that post: his 130 days as a special government employee ran out and on 26 March 2026 he became co-chair of the President's Council of Advisors on Science and Technology (PCAST), an advisory body (source 12). On 19 Sept 2026 President Trump said he would name a new AI czar; nobody had been named as of 20 Sept 2026.

**Official vs personal.** Sources 8, 9 and 10 date from his time in office and state administration policy; source 9 is a government document he co-signed. Sources 0–5 and 11 post-date his departure and are his own voice, although he remains an adviser to the President. In practice the two are indistinguishable on every item here; no prediction rests on the official documents alone.

## 0. X — "Dario has written that we need to 'pace the frontier'… go ahead" (13 Sept 2026)
https://x.com/DavidSacks/status/2098973625252708460

His reply to Dario Amodei's September 2026 essay and Sam Altman's agreement with it; the single most useful source.

- "Dario has written that we need to 'pace the frontier,' and Sam has agreed. People may be surprised by my response: go ahead."
- "You guys are the frontier. By any reasonable metric — market share, revenue growth, model capability — the two of you have a duopoly on frontier intelligence."
- "I don't see what you see in the lab. If the unreleased models are scary enough that you think you should slow down, I support your decision to be responsible."
- "But stop pretending you need anyone else's permission. Stop pretending antitrust law has to be suspended so you can form a cartel. Stop pretending you need a regulatory approval process that supersedes product liability. Stop pretending METR is independent… Stop pretending you need those same evaluators to police competitors who aren't even at the frontier."
- "You face massive product-liability exposure if your products enable a truly damaging cyberattack. The market already punishes models that behave in unpredictable or unauthorized ways. After the Hugging Face episode, it is simply good business for OpenAI and Anthropic to trade some raw power for reliability and predictability. Call it alignment if you want. It is also just giving customers what they want."
- "Pacing the frontier would also create breathing room for a more intelligent conversation about regulation than Bernie Sanders' 'shut it all down.' China is very unlikely to join a global agreement, as you know, and that has to be taken into account as well."
- "The easiest way not to build superintelligence is for you to agree not to build it. Demanding your preferred regulatory framework as the price of that will look like blackmail of the public and the political system."
- "If you don't, we'll know this was just another bid for regulatory capture — or an election-season psyop."

Used for d5, d9, s4, s6, s8, s13, p4. Note for s13: this is why he is not at 100. He accepts, even invites, voluntary slowing by private firms; what he rejects is slowing imposed or brokered by government.

## 9. The White House — Winning the Race: America's AI Action Plan (23 July 2025)

## Where he is uncertain, or where we are

- **What the labs see.** His most hedged sentence is "I don't see what you see in the lab." He does not claim to know that unreleased models are harmless; he claims that if they are dangerous the builders can and must hold back themselves. That is the reason s13 is 82 rather than 100 and s8 is 12 rather than 0.
- **"This could change."** The Goldilocks post is explicitly a snapshot ("The AI race is highly dynamic so this could change… there is no guarantee that this continues"), and in Sept 2026 he concedes that "prosaic" AI-assisted AI research is happening. d3 is low but not at the floor.
- **No numbers.** He has never given a probability of catastrophe or an AGI date. d1 is "high" because he rejects the end-of-humanity prediction in so many words, not because he has rejected the specific 1-in-10 bar.
- **p2 (20-year horizon)** is the weakest prediction. His job-loss scepticism is about the present ("middle-to-middle"); "as overhyped as AGI itself" suggests long timelines but he has not said so for 2046. His 2023 All-In remarks on AutoGPT and AGI (episode E124, April 2023) could not be retrieved and may have been more bullish on capability.
- **s10 (lives saved)**: he argues from the China race, the economy and optimism, not from cures. The value is an inference from his general opposition to slowing.
- **s4 (treaty)**: no direct statement on a nuclear-style inspected treaty was found. The prediction rests on "China is very unlikely to join a global agreement", his rejection of outside evaluators, and the administration's refusal to sign the Paris declaration (Feb 2025).
- **d6**: he would agree that the next decade's harms are not catastrophes, but he also plays down the "familiar" harms (job loss "overhyped"). The harm he stresses is political: censorship through state-corporate fusion.

## Verification notes

- **Title as of Sept 2026**: not AI czar. Co-chair of PCAST since 26 March 2026 (TechCrunch, CNBC, The Hill, TIME). CBS says "May"; treated as an error. He still advises the President informally (Vox, 18 Sept 2026, via Yahoo: https://www.yahoo.com/news/politics/articles/adviser-urging-trump-reject-ai-100000580.html).
- **Misattributions caught**: (a) the "agent swarms… against anyone's wishes" sentence is Bilal Chughtai's, not Sacks's; (b) "It's a false premise… we have no control" is Jason Calacanis's. Neither is used.
- **Second-hand quotes** (post not retrieved): source 5 ("A pause would not make America safer…") and source 11 (open-source "duopoly" reply). "Overly dramatic" (d1) comes from a secondary write-up of the CBS extended video. Each prediction that cites them also rests on a first-hand quote.
- **Reaction to the July 2026 OpenAI agent incident**: his only located comment is in source 0 ("After the Hugging Face episode, it is simply good business… to trade some raw power for reliability"). A secondary write-up says he blamed lab negligence (sandbox misconfiguration) in the CBS interview; not verified against the video.
- **Reaction to the July 2026 "Pacing the Frontier" letter**: no statement from July–August 2026 was found; source 0 (13 Sept) is his first located response to the pacing idea.
- **TIME100 AI 2026** says he advised postponing an executive order requiring government testing of models and that a revised version with a shorter review window was later signed. Not independently verified, and the EO text was not read; it would not change s6, since he opposed the requirement.
- **Washington Examiner** (13 Sept 2026) headlines source 0 as backing a "self-imposed AI slowdown"; accurate as far as it goes, but he backs it only for the two leading labs and only as their own choice.
- **Not retrieved**: NPR on EO 14365 (timed out), CNN on the AI Force (HTTP 451), Bloomberg originals (paywalled), the 2023 All-In episodes, his speeches at the July 2025 "Winning the AI Race" summit.
- **Conflict of interest**, often raised by critics (Gizmodo, Vox): he is an active venture investor with AI holdings. Noted for the record; it does not bear on predicting his stated views.
