# A. Scott Alexander

## 0. Astral Codex Ten — My AI Opinions (11 June 2026)
https://www.astralcodexten.com/p/my-ai-opinions

His most recent and most complete statement; most predictions rest on it.

- "Define AGI as AI intelligent enough to do 90% of knowledge work jobs. I think there's a 25% chance of AGI by 2027, a 50% chance by 2034, and a 75% chance by 2045."
- Diffusion gap (AGI exists → AI actually does half of knowledge work): "I think there's a 25% chance the diffusion gap is less than 3 years, and a 50% chance it's less than 10 years."
- "I think there's a 25% chance the superhuman gap range will be less than 1 year, a 50% chance it will last less than 4 years, and a 75% chance it will last less than 10 years."
- On recursive self-improvement: "This is one of the biggest blank spots in my model… It could fizzle out completely for lack of compute, or it could go vertical."
- "Define the point of no return as the point where, if an AI wanted to eliminate humanity, humans would no longer have a plausible chance of stopping it… 25% chance the gap between AGI and the point of no return will be less than 3 years, a 50% chance it will be less than 10 years."
- "My modal scenario looks something like AGI in 2031, which diffuses throughout the economy until more than half of jobs are automated by the late 2030s… the point of no return is sometime around then [early 2040s]."
- "If corporations only pursued safety to the degree encouraged by normal corporate incentives, I think there's a 50% chance that the first AIs to cross the point of no return would want to eliminate the human population."
- "Given the current amount that corporations are pursuing safety, I think there's a 20% chance that the first AIs to cross the point of no return will want to eliminate the human population."
- "I think there's an additional 30% chance that they otherwise permanently curtail human potential, either for their own reasons (they were partially misaligned), or because they're aligned to a regime with abhorrent values, or because something goes wrong on the way to ASI."
- "I think there's a 50% chance we get a warning shot before AI crosses the point of no return."
- "I find myself more optimistic about alignment than the average person who thinks about AI safety at all (although still more pessimistic than the average member of the population)… Unfortunately, this is probably because I don't understand these techniques well enough to have fully grasped their flaws."
- "I think the Swiss cheese is in a good direction here - I can think of lots of stories for how we win, and misalignment needs to survive all of them - but again, most of the smartest alignment researchers are much more pessimistic."
- "I think there's about a 40% chance that the US and China will agree to a well-designed AI pause (as above) sometime before AI crosses the point of no return."
- "A good pause strategy would involve both sides being able to monitor the other's data centers to prevent illegal training, then limiting training to some slow mutually-agreeable rate that lets alignment researchers thoroughly test each generation of AIs pre-release, monitor them post-release… I'm less optimistic about a true pause/stop as opposed to a slowdown, because at some point technology and algorithmic progress advance to a point where it's too easy for small groups outside the control of the US and China to race ahead. I think a good pause like this could buy 20-50 years."
- "I'm nervous about the strongest forms of pause activism… Still, I think probably we've passed the point in political salience where the marginal unit of pro-pause activism from someone outside that actor set is beneficial on net, and I'm nervously but committedly supporting it."
- "Before I even thought pausing was a realistic option, I said I had a 20% p(doom)… I think my real p(doom|no pause) is probably a few points above 20%, and my real p(doom|maybe-pause) is probably a few points below 20%… I'm rounding both of them off to 20%."
- "If we had a 30 year pause, I think the chance within that 30 year period that we destroy ourselves with bioweapons is 5%, nukes 5%, and some sort of complexity catastrophe… 5%. That's almost as high as my p(doom) from AI! I still think it probably comes out net positive, partly because it might decrease the risk of some of the sub-existential catastrophes like dictatorship, and partly because the pause might not last thirty years, and shorter pause lengths (like five years) seem like no-brainers."
- On a corporate power grab: "When I try to game the corporate version of this, I can't make it hang together… The government version is scarier." And: "I am worried about concentration of power, though less worried than I might otherwise be."
- "I expect that closed-source AIs will be heavily optimized against helping with [bioweapons], and open-source AI will be banned after the first warning shot (or become economically prohibitive even before then)."
- Footnote: "Did I change my mind since AI 2027? No: I said at the time that my timelines were a bit longer than the median member of the group."

## Where Scott Alexander is uncertain
He says so himself at length. The biggest "blank spot" in his model is recursive self-improvement, which drives d3: he will not say a sudden jump is probable, only that it is possible enough to plan for (25% under one year). On alignment (d5, d9) he admits his relative optimism may come from not understanding the techniques' flaws and gives "about equal evidentiary value" to pessimistic experts and optimistic common sense, so both sit near the middle. d6 is an inference from timelines (point of no return about 2040, 50% odds of a warning shot) and gets low confidence. On s1 he is torn: a long pause is "probably net positive", yet "if we never get AI… the future [is] short and grim" and a true stop is hard to enforce. s5 is a real tension in his writing (keep the US lead through export controls; do not race; seek a mutual deal), so 50. s6: he wants pre-release testing, safety cases and audits, but has never endorsed an FDA-style licence and is a long-standing critic of the FDA. p2 combines his own AGI and diffusion distributions; the combined 62% is my arithmetic, not his number.

https://stevenpinker.com/files/pinker/files/possibleminds_pinker.pdf

## Where Steven Pinker is uncertain (or silent)
His view of the danger axis is said almost verbatim and has not moved from 2018 to September 2026, so d1, d3, d5, d6 and d7 are high. What he has conceded is capability: "I underestimated what AI was about to be capable of" (July 2026), and in 2020 he told Russell "perhaps I have overestimated the common sense of the AI community". He has said very little about specific policy. No statement was found on an AI treaty (s4), the geopolitical race (s5), licensing or pre-release approval (s6), or open weights (s11); those are low-confidence inferences, with s5 and s11 placed at the midpoint because his stated principles point both ways. s6 is pulled down by his GMO and nuclear "overregulated out of worst-case fears" argument and up by "test the living daylights out of any system". s13 (65) reflects that he opposes slowing out of doom fears but has never argued for maximum speed and praises safety regulation that learns from accidents. p2 is low confidence: his older statements say jobs will not go quickly and AGI is incoherent, but those predate his 2026 admission. s1, s8 and s10 are med: he was never asked those questions, but his premise (negligible catastrophe risk, real costs of technophobia) fixes the answers.
